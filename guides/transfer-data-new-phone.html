Switching Phones? How to Transfer Everything Without Losing Data

By the Everyday Tech Help editorial team · Published September 27, 2026

A new phone is exciting until you think about everything living on the old one: thousands of photos, years of messages, all your apps and logins. The good news is that moving everything over is far easier than it used to be — both Apple and Google have built dedicated tools for exactly this. Follow this guide in order and nothing important gets left behind.

Before you start: the pre-move checklist

Do these on your old phone before touching the new one:

  1. Update everything. Install any pending software updates on the old phone — transfers work best when both phones run recent software.
  2. Back up. On iPhone: Settings → [your name] → iCloud → iCloud Backup → Back Up Now. On Android: Settings → Google → Backup → Back up now. This is your safety net if anything goes wrong.
  3. Charge both phones to at least 50%, or keep them plugged in during the transfer.
  4. Connect to Wi-Fi. Transfers can move tens of gigabytes — you don't want that on mobile data.
  5. Know your passwords. You'll need your Apple ID or Google account password, your phone's passcode, and ideally the passwords for your most important apps.
  6. Turn off two-factor prompts tied to the old phone number if you're also changing your number — or keep the old phone active until the new one is fully set up.

iPhone to iPhone: Quick Start

Apple's Quick Start is genuinely excellent:

  1. Turn on the new iPhone and place it next to your old iPhone.
  2. The old iPhone will show a "Set Up New iPhone" prompt — tap Continue.
  3. An animation appears on the new phone; hold the old phone's camera over it to pair them (like scanning a QR code).
  4. Enter your old phone's passcode on the new phone, then follow the prompts to set up Face ID or Touch ID.
  5. Choose Transfer from iPhone to copy everything directly, phone to phone. Keep them next to each other and plugged in — this can take 30 minutes to a few hours depending on how much you have.

Alternatively, choose Restore from iCloud Backup if you don't have the old phone handy — you'll need the backup from the checklist above.

Android to Android: the cable or wireless transfer

  1. Turn on the new Android phone and connect it to Wi-Fi.
  2. When prompted, sign in with your Google account — use the same account as your old phone.
  3. You'll be offered a copy from your old phone option. If you have a USB cable that fits both phones (often included in the box, or use an adapter), the cable method is fastest and most complete.
  4. On the old phone, confirm the transfer and choose what to copy: apps, photos, contacts, messages, and settings.
  5. Keep both phones close together and plugged in until it's done.

Samsung phones have their own tool called Smart Switch (pre-installed), which works the same way and can even pull from an iPhone.

Switching between iPhone and Android

Cross-platform moves take a little more care, but the essentials transfer fine:

ContactsSync via your Google account — they appear automaticallyUse Apple's Move to iOS app on the old Android during setup
PhotosInstall Google Photos on the iPhone first and let it back everything upMove to iOS transfers camera photos
MessagesWhatsApp has its own built-in transfer; SMS history is the tricky one — check your carrier's optionsMove to iOS transfers message history
AppsYou'll reinstall them; paid apps usually need repurchasingSame — check which of your apps exist on iOS
WhatsApp chatsUse WhatsApp's Settings → Chats → Move chats to AndroidUse Move chats to iOS during setup

Important if leaving iPhone: turn off iMessage before removing your SIM (Settings → Messages → iMessage → off). If you skip this, texts from iPhone-using friends may vanish into iMessage limbo for days.

After the transfer: the verification round

Don't wipe the old phone yet. On the new phone, check:

  • Photos: scroll back to your oldest photos — are they all there?
  • Contacts: spot-check a few, especially ones added recently.
  • Messages: open your most important conversations.
  • Apps: open your banking, email, and authenticator apps. Authenticator apps in particular often need to be re-set-up rather than transferred — do this while you still have the old phone.
  • Make a test call and send a text to confirm the SIM and network work.

Live with the new phone for a few days before wiping the old one. Only when you're confident everything is there should you factory-reset the old phone (Settings → General → Transfer or Reset → Erase All Content on iPhone; Settings → System → Reset → Erase all data on Android) — especially if you're selling it or giving it away.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the transfer take?

Anywhere from 20 minutes to several hours, depending on how much data you have. Photos are the biggest factor. Start it when you don't need either phone — overnight works well.

Will my apps still be logged in?

Some will, most won't. Expect to log back into banking, email, social media, and shopping apps. This is normal and actually good security practice. A password manager makes this painless.

What about my two-factor authentication codes?

This is the one people get bitten by. Authenticator apps don't always transfer cleanly. Before wiping the old phone, open each important account's security settings on the new phone and confirm your codes work — or temporarily switch to SMS codes during the move.

Can I transfer if my old phone's screen is broken?

If the phone still powers on and you previously enabled cloud backup, you can restore from that backup without touching the old phone. If there was no backup and the screen is dead, a repair shop may be able to extract the data — back up regularly to avoid this situation.
