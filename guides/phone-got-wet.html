Phone Got Wet? What to Do in the First 10 Minutes

By the Everyday Tech Help editorial team · Published September 27, 2026

Your phone just took an unexpected swim — in the sink, the toilet, a puddle, or the pool. Your heart is pounding and your first instinct is to check if it still works. Stop. What you do in the next few minutes matters more than anything else, and most people do exactly the wrong thing. This guide walks you through the steps that give your phone the best chance of surviving, in plain language, no technical background needed.

Going deeper: Topics Quest has published a detailed guide on getting water out of a phone, with extra drying methods and model-specific tips.

The first 60 seconds: get it out and power it down

Speed matters here, but calm speed. Every second your phone sits in liquid, water creeps deeper inside through the charging port, speaker holes, and SIM tray.

  1. Fish it out immediately. Don't pause to find a towel first — just get it out of the water.
  2. Turn it off right away if it's still on. Hold the power button and shut it down. If the screen is already black, leave it that way — do not try to turn it on to "check."
  3. Remove the case and anything attached to it (pop socket, wallet case, charger cable). Cases trap water against the phone.
  4. Take out the SIM card tray if you can. On most phones there's a tiny hole on the side; the little metal pin that came in your phone box opens it. This lets air circulate inside and lets you rescue your SIM card. If you can't find the pin, a straightened paperclip works.

Why power it down? Electricity and water are the combination that kills phones. A powered-off phone has far less current running through it, which means less chance of a short circuit frying the insides.

What to do in the first 10 minutes

Once the phone is out and off, follow these steps in order:

  1. Hold it upright and gently shake out excess water. Port facing down, give it a few light shakes — think of shaking water out of an umbrella, not shaking a cocktail.
  2. Dab, don't rub. Use a soft, dry cloth or paper towel to pat the outside dry. Rubbing can push water deeper into openings.
  3. Remove anything removable. SIM tray out (already done), and if your phone has a removable back cover or memory card slot, open those too.
  4. Stand it upright in a dry, ventilated spot — leaning against a wall with the charging port facing down works well. Gravity is your friend; you want water to drain out, not pool inside.
  5. Leave it alone. This is the hardest step. Do not press buttons, do not plug it in, do not try to turn it on "just to see."

What NOT to do (the mistakes that kill phones)

  • Don't use a hair dryer. The heat can melt seals and warp internal parts. Even the "cool" setting blows water deeper inside.
  • Don't put it in the microwave. This should go without saying, but people try it. It will destroy the phone and is dangerous.
  • Don't charge it. Plugging in a wet phone can cause a short circuit. Wait until you're confident it's fully dry — that means at least 24 to 48 hours.
  • Don't blow into the ports with your mouth. Your breath is moist and just adds more water.
  • Don't tap "it's probably fine" and keep using it. Water damage often shows up days later as corrosion. A phone that seems fine today can die next week.

How to dry your phone properly

Forget the old advice about burying your phone in a bag of rice. Rice doesn't absorb moisture from inside a phone effectively, and the dust and starch can get into the ports and cause their own problems. Here are better options:

Option 1: Silica gel packets (best)

Those little "do not eat" packets that come in shoe boxes and vitamin bottles are silica gel, and they're genuinely good at absorbing moisture. Put your phone in a sealed container or zip-lock bag with several packets and leave it for 24 to 48 hours.

Option 2: A fan and fresh air

Stand the phone upright in front of a fan on a cool setting. Moving air evaporates moisture steadily and safely. Leave it for at least 24 hours, 48 if the phone was fully submerged.

Option 3: A dry, warm (not hot) room

A sunny windowsill or a warm room works, but avoid direct hot sunlight for long periods. The goal is gentle warmth with good airflow.

After 24 to 48 hours: the moment of truth

Once the phone has had a full day or two to dry:

  1. Inspect the charging port and other openings with a flashlight. If you see moisture or fogging, give it more time.
  2. Put the SIM tray back in and try turning the phone on.
  3. If it powers up, test the essentials: touchscreen, speakers, microphone (make a call), camera, and charging.
  4. Charge it only after you've confirmed it turns on and seems dry. Use a wall charger and keep an eye on it the first time — if it gets unusually hot, unplug it.

When to get professional help

Take the phone to a repair shop if any of these apply:

  • It won't turn on after 48 hours of drying.
  • It turns on but the screen has lines, spots, or doesn't respond to touch.
  • It was submerged in salt water, soapy water, or anything other than clean fresh water. These leave residue that keeps corroding parts even after drying — a technician can clean the internals properly.
  • It charges erratically or gets hot.

Be honest with the technician about what happened. Most phones have small moisture indicator stickers inside that change color when wet, so they'll know anyway — and knowing the full story helps them fix it.

Preventing the next accident

  • A waterproof pouch costs very little and is worth it for beach days, pool trips, and boating.
  • Keep your phone out of back pockets near toilets and out of bathroom counters near sinks.
  • Check your phone's water resistance rating (many modern phones are rated IP67 or IP68), but remember: water resistance wears down over time and doesn't cover all liquids.

Frequently asked questions

My phone was only splashed, not submerged. Do I still need to do all this?

A light splash on the screen is usually fine — just dry it off. But if water got into the charging port or speaker openings, power it down and give it several hours to dry before charging. When in doubt, wait.

Can I use the wireless charger instead of the cable?

Not until the phone is fully dry. Wireless charging still generates heat and electrical activity. Wait the full 24 to 48 hours either way.

The phone works fine now. Am I in the clear?

Mostly, but keep an eye on it for a week or two. Corrosion can develop slowly. If the battery starts draining faster than usual or the speakers sound muffled, have it checked.

Does my warranty cover water damage?

Usually not, even on water-resistant phones. Manufacturers treat water damage as accidental damage. Check whether you have accidental damage coverage or phone insurance — that's what covers this.
